1. CONFIRMED PROBLEMS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The forensic audit of lesson 715 (§2.1) confirmed two general architecture problems.

PROBLEM A — PHYSICAL READ RANGE ≠ LESSON SEMANTIC RANGE

Current behavior effectively treats every verified block on selected physical pages as eligible lesson content.

Example:

§2.1 uses physical pages 26–29.

Page 29 also contains the beginning of adjacent material:

"ՏԵՂԱՇԱՐԺ ԿՈՈՐԴԻՆԱՏԱՅԻՆ ԱՌԱՆՑՔԻ ԵՐԿԱՅՆՔՈՎ"

and exercise 87.

Because they are physically on page 29, they entered Pass 2 and produced a canonical MicroNode even though they belong to adjacent/next instructional material.

Source provenance was correct.
Lesson semantic scope was not.

PROBLEM B — TARGET DISCOVERY IS STILL CANDIDATE-GATED

Current IndependentLearningTarget discovery starts from candidateSnapshots.

Therefore:

verified source
        ↓
Pass 2 candidate exists?
        ↓
YES → target may exist
NO  → target cannot independently exist

This means candidate generation still defines what knowledge is visible to completeness.

Exercises/outcomes/source evidence can enrich an existing candidate but cannot independently expose a missing learning target.

That is not sufficient.

7. SOURCE GROUNDING RULE
════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Do NOT fabricate curriculum targets from exercises alone.

A target seed must be grounded in legitimate IN_SCOPE lesson evidence.

However, source grounding may be distributed.

For example:

- instructional statement/context establishes the concept/performance family;
- multiple exercises establish that it is independently assessed.

Exercise clusters may therefore DISCOVER or strengthen a target when they are semantically grounded by the lesson source.

This is different from blindly converting every exercise into a target.

One exercise ≠ one MicroNode.

One outcome ≠ one MicroNode.

One source block ≠ one MicroNode.

The unit is:

ONE independently assessable learner state.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════
8. ATOMICITY / LEARNER-STATE RULE
════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Two concepts/performances should remain separate targets when a learner can realistically master one and fail the other.

Use this as the primary atomicity test:

"Could the learner demonstrate mastery of A while failing B?"

If YES:
they should normally remain independently auditable targets.

Examples:

DOMAIN vs RANGE

A learner may correctly determine domain but incorrectly determine range.

Therefore they must remain independently visible in the Target Ledger.

GRAPH → DOMAIN
and
GRAPH → RANGE

must not disappear into a generic:

"analyze function graph"

unless explicit certified canonical logic intentionally represents both learner states and preserves separate evidence semantics.

Similarly:

FIND x FOR GIVEN y

is not equivalent to:

FIND ZEROS

because zero is only the special case y = 0.

Likewise:

IDENTIFY ZEROS

is not equivalent to:

CONSTRUCT GRAPH FROM CONSTRAINTS.
